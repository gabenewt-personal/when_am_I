# Narrative / editorial guide

The premise: the player wakes without knowing *when* they are. Every clue continues **one lived scene**, not a disconnected fact list. Use second-person present tense, economical sensory prose, and avoid calling out the answer before clue 6.

## Six-beat progression

1. **Disorientation** — a vivid, plausible sensory detail that could fit multiple answers.
2. **Environment** — texture, architecture, sound, vegetation or habits that begin to narrow time.
3. **First signature** — a genuine but still ambiguous historical/geological marker.
4. **Reconsideration** — a strong clue forcing the player to revise their mental image.
5. **Recognition** — a highly distinctive marker, answer likely for informed players.
6. **Near certainty** — date range or unmistakable historical/geological identifier, without naming the correct answer outright.

## Review checklist

- Does the prompt announce exactly what kind of answer is wanted?
- Is every clue historically/scientifically compatible with the scene's internal anchor year/location?
- Could a discerning player identify the answer from clue 2–4, or does every scene only become solvable at clue 6?
- Is clue 1 vague but interesting rather than generic filler?
- Are clues presented one at a time in the same evolving environment?
- Could any art, alt text, asset filename, API payload display or autocomplete sorting leak the answer prematurely?
- Is the full answer list canonical, complete, searchable, and mutually exclusive at the selected level?
- Is the after-game epilogue educational, brief, and factual without flattening the story into a lecture?
- Are references included for human fact-checking? A source link supports a factual claim but does not automatically license images or text copying.

## Future expansion

A separate `Where Am I?` mode could introduce `U.S. National Parks`, `U.S. National Monuments`, `Mountain Ranges`, etc. Its answer pool must be curated at the required specificity, and historic units/designations must use clear dates. Initially, keep only the When prototype to test clue pacing and replay intent.
